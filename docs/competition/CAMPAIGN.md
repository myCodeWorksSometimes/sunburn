# Embers of Before — Complete Campaign Story and Mechanics

**Status:** **Adopted by the user on 17 October 2026 as the target story and gameplay design.** It supersedes the story beats in NARRATIVE-SCRIPT.md and the "Story" section of ACTS-IMPLEMENTATION.md wherever they disagree. CANON.md has been updated to match. The current build only partly implements it: see **"Integration with the current build"** and **"MVP status"** at the end of this file.

**Prepared:** 17 October 2026. **Adopted:** 17 October 2026.

**Target:** A 25–30 minute first playthrough, with two bosses, one elite encounter, exploration, environmental puzzles and one optional task. Durations below are design budgets that require playtesting.

This campaign makes the people Rowan helps give him the knowledge and routes he needs to rescue Eli. Searching, puzzles, fights and story discoveries form one chain.

## The story in one paragraph

After a meteor corrupts the world into endless night, Rowan searches for his younger brother using a lantern that briefly restores places to their healthy past. Along the way, he helps a family whom Eli helped evacuate. Their experiences reveal that Eli reached the clock tower, then stayed behind to draw shadows away from the escaping children. Rowan finds him alive, trapped in a fractured moment. To bring him back, Rowan must spend the last memory ember of their home, permanently losing the ability to revisit its remembered afternoon. He fights through the tower, anchors Eli into the present, and finally shares the promised view with him. The world remains dark. His brother comes home.

## The rules that keep everything coherent

1. **The meteor caused corruption and endless night.** People and creatures can still move. Some locations and people have become caught in damaged fragments of time.
2. **Restoration temporarily returns a local place to a specific healthy, pre-impact moment.** Different fires can recall different moments.
3. **Recorded people cannot respond to new actions.** Living survivors can. This distinction becomes essential when finding Eli.
4. **Present-day evidence reveals events after the impact.** Footprints, witness accounts, broken equipment and abandoned belongings tell the evacuation story.
5. **Physical changes made in the present can persist.** Opened gates, released ladders and secured walkways remain available.
6. **A family hearth’s memory ember can anchor a living member of that household.** Taking its core permanently spends that hearth’s restoration. Ordinary fire and resting still work.
7. **Anchoring does not resurrect dead people or cure the world.**
8. **Shadows are creatures of corruption, separate from the villagers.** Defeating one never secretly kills a person.

The hearth rule is established through another survivor’s experience before Rowan needs it. There is no ancient inscription conveniently predicting this disaster.

## The people

- **Rowan:** Eli’s older brother. Practical, protective and accustomed to postponing shared time until his work is finished. His change is from assuming what Eli needs to listening and acting.
- **Eli:** Nine. Playful, observant and frightened by the disaster. He knows the village’s shortcuts and helps other children because he recognises them—not because he has supernatural knowledge.
- **Mara:** Runs the market’s toy-and-game stall. She taught Eli card tricks. Her daughter is Nia; her partner is Tomas.
- **Tomas:** The bridge keeper. He let Eli help with small repairs. He has survived being trapped in corrupted time and can explain what brought him back.
- **Nia:** Six. Eli’s friend. She witnessed the evacuation and provides a child’s account of what happened.

Mara, Tomas and Nia are one family. Helping them creates a relationship that continues through the game.

## The geography

The main route is:

**Home → Market → Bridge Refuge → Broken Bridge → Playground → Canal → Clock Tower**

An **Old Road connects the tower to the Bridge Refuge**. Its evacuation gate can initially be opened only from the tower side. Children escaped through it, but it fell shut afterward.

The player sees its closed western entrance early. Opening it later creates a recognisable shortcut for the hearth return and ending.

This is one connected world. Chapter names mark changes in the adventure; they do not reset the scenery into separate stationary levels.

## What happened before the player wakes

This chronology is for development. The game reveals it gradually.

- Eli was at the playground when the meteor struck.
- He helped younger children reach the market’s gathering point.
- Mara directed them toward the designated shelter at the clock tower, then went to find Tomas.
- Tomas had become caught in corrupted time near the bridge controls. Mara brought an ember from their household hearth. It allowed him to cross back into the present, but their hearth lost its remembered world.
- When corruption reached the tower shelter, Eli helped the children escape through the Old Road. He rang the tower bell to pull approaching shadows away from their exit.
- The children reached Tomas at the Bridge Refuge. Eli became trapped on the tower’s upper landing.
- Mara went back to the market for supplies and became trapped inside her stall.
- Rowan wakes in the cellar.

Nobody knows the whole story immediately. Each witness tells what they actually saw.

## Scene 1 — The unfinished promise

**Target:** Opening 20–30 seconds.

An ordinary afternoon. Rowan is carrying supplies toward the cellar. Eli waits by the door, looking toward the tower.

> **Eli:** “The tower today?”  
> **Rowan:** “After work. I promise.”

Eli taps twice on the doorframe. Rowan answers with two taps on the wood he is carrying.

Eli leaves for the playground.

The impact happens while Rowan is below ground. Dust falls, ordinary sounds stop, and the screen goes dark.

**Purpose:** Establish the relationship, their signal, the destination and why they were separated. Rowan’s postponed promise did not cause the disaster.

## Scene 2 — The house remembers

**Target:** Minutes 0–3, including the opening.

**Objective:** Find Eli.

The player wakes in the cellar, collects the lantern and climbs upstairs.

The house is damaged. The front route is obstructed, but the hearth can be awakened.

**Player actions:**

- Learn movement and climbing.
- Take the lantern.
- Use restoration at the hearth.
- Climb the temporarily restored stairs to release the front-door latch.

Restoration returns daylight, an intact roof and moving leaves outside.

The departure scene repeats exactly.

> **Eli’s recording:** “The tower today?”  
> **Remembered Rowan:** “After work. I promise.”

Present-day Rowan calls to Eli. The recording continues without answering.

When restoration ends, the house returns to ruin. The released front-door latch remains open.

**What the player learns:** Memories are fixed, temporary geometry can provide a route, and present actions can create permanent progress.

**Exit:** Rowan leaves toward the market.

## Scene 3 — The village lane

**Target:** Minutes 3–4.

**Objective:** Reach the market.

A Crawler blocks a narrow route. Its anticipation animation clearly precedes its attack.

There is a safe fire before the encounter and a raised route beside it.

**Player choices:**

- Fight with two correctly aimed lantern sweeps.
- Use the raised route to avoid it.
- Retreat to recover and try again.

A hanging metal sign can be struck through a contextual interaction. Its sound makes the Crawler investigate the sign.

**Important setup:** Shadows react to loud disturbances. Later, Eli’s use of the tower bell will follow an established behaviour.

Basic movement and illumination consume no flame. The player is learning decisions, not fighting a constant fuel countdown.

## Scene 4 — Mara’s shuttered stall

**Target:** Minutes 4–6.

**Objective:** Free the person inside the stall.

Rowan hears knocking from behind a damaged shutter. Mara is trapped inside, protected by a small ordinary light.

The square’s eastern exit is covered by tendrils belonging to a creature rooted in her card-game counter.

**Environmental puzzle:**

- Inspect the shutter and notice its release on the roof.
- Restore the awning, creating a climbable route.
- Reach the roof release.
- Remove the physical obstruction from the counterweight.

The shutter opens permanently. Mara can emerge into the stall’s protected area, but the creature still blocks the route out of the square.

A short market recording shows Eli playing Mara’s five-card game on an earlier day.

> **Recorded Eli:** “You moved your hand. The card stayed there.”

This is an observation lesson, not a conversation with the present.

Mara confirms that Eli helped Nia and the other children leave.

> **Mara:** “He came back for the little ones. Took them toward the bridge.”

**New objective:** Clear the market exit.

## Scene 5 — Boss one: Five-Hand

**Target:** Minutes 6–8.

Five-Hand is a mass of shadow tangled around the game counter. It uses five painted wooden cards to conceal the vulnerable knot sustaining its shield.

It does not ask Rowan to gamble. **Tracking the correct card exposes something the player can attack.**

**The encounter has three repeating steps:**

1. **Observe.** The cards turn face up. The vulnerable knot appears beneath the lantern card.
2. **Track and select.** The cards turn over and undergo visible swaps. The player chooses which one to expose.
3. **React.** A correct selection opens an attack window. A wrong selection triggers a telegraphed hand sweep that can be dodged.

Two successful exposures and lantern hits defeat it.

**Round one:** Two slow swaps.  
**Round two:** Three swaps, including one crossing movement.

**Fairness rules:**

- The swaps are genuinely trackable.
- The creature never secretly changes the answer.
- No attacks occur while the player must watch or select.
- Wrong answers create a combat consequence rather than unavoidable damage.
- A missed attack window allows another attempt.
- A slower-shuffle option changes presentation speed without changing the solution.

The card interaction itself costs no flame. Successful lantern attacks use the normal attack cost.

Defeating Five-Hand disperses the tendrils. The market exit remains clear.

Mara heads toward the Bridge Refuge. Rowan follows through the newly opened route.

**First major payoff:** The player has rescued someone, learned a skill and used it against a distinctive opponent.

## Scene 6 — The family at the refuge

**Target:** Minutes 8–10.

**Objective:** Ask about Eli and find a route across.

Mara discovers Nia safe with Tomas. Their brief reunion is a direct consequence of the player freeing her.

The other evacuated children are accounted for. Eli is the missing one.

Nia explains:

> **Nia:** “We went to the tower. Then the shadows came.”  
> **Nia:** “Eli said to use the old road when the bell rang.”  
> **Rowan:** “Did he come with you?”  
> **Nia:** “We heard it. He didn’t come.”

The player now has a clear destination. The uncertainty concerns Eli’s condition and how to reach him.

The Old Road’s western gate is visibly shut. Tomas explains that its release is on the tower side.

The main bridge is the available route.

**The hearth rule is established here.**

The refuge’s household hearth provides ordinary warmth, but cannot restore its old room. Tomas is physically present beside it.

> **Tomas:** “The bridge fire could show me. I couldn’t get out.”  
> **Mara:** “I brought an ember from our hearth. Then he could take my hand.”  
> **Tomas:** “The house hasn’t remembered since.”

The player can inspect the spent hearth. Its ordinary rest function works; restoration does not.

This gives Rowan—and the player—evidence for a possible rescue method without proving that Eli is still alive.

## Scene 7 — The bridge works

**Target:** Minutes 10–13.

**Objective:** Release the bridge mechanism.

Tomas is injured and cannot reach the maintenance controls. He explains where they are and provides a simple diagram.

The task takes place beside the actual bridge. It is not a detached errand in an unrelated room.

### First obstruction: the Braced Shadow

This elite creature has a protected front. It prepares a charge with a long, readable wind-up.

The player must:

- Position near a cracked maintenance barrier.
- Bait the charge.
- Dodge.
- Attack its exposed rear during recovery.

The collision also breaks the barrier, opening the maintenance bay.

### Second obstruction: the linked gears

Restoration makes the damaged machinery operational.

- Lever A rotates both gears.
- Lever B rotates only the smaller gear.
- Clear markers show the required alignment.
- Once aligned, the player locks the axle with the present-day brake.

The secured brake persists when restoration ends.

### Final challenge: the crossing

Awakening the bridge fire restores its deck for a short traversal window. The timer begins only when the route is usable.

On the far bank, the player releases a maintenance walkway that remains usable in the present.

**Result:** The outward crossing is earned through combat and a puzzle. Returning never depends on surviving another expiring bridge.

## Scene 8 — The playground investigation

**Target:** Minutes 13–16.

**Objective:** Find the service route to the canal.

The obvious exit is buried. The treehouse contains an old maintenance access.

This section combines searching and movement:

- Present-day footprints establish where the children gathered.
- A pre-impact recording shows Eli using the treehouse’s service hatch during ordinary play.
- Restoration starts the swing moving.
- The player rides it to reach the treehouse platform.

The player finds **Eli’s drawing and the family’s metal ember cup**.

The cup is a practical, heatproof carrier that Eli used during his playground expeditions. It is not a magical object that works only for a chosen hero.

The drawing shows Rowan and Eli standing together at the tower railing.

The cup is required for safely carrying the hearth ember. The drawing supplies the emotional connection and is recorded in the journal.

### Combat variation: the Pouncer

It watches from a perch and marks its intended landing through posture and a visible cue. It attacks where the player was standing.

The player can evade it or counterattack after it lands. Standing still and repeatedly attacking is ineffective.

### Optional task: Nia’s wooden bird

Nia mentioned leaving her wooden bird at the playground. A small side ledge contains it and supplies. Returning it during the hearth journey provides additional dialogue and a supplies reward, subject to inventory caps.

No optional object blocks the rescue.

## Scene 9 — The canal machinery

**Target:** Minutes 16–18.

**Objective:** Reach the tower courtyard.

The canal’s old waterwheel powers a maintenance lift. In the corrupted present, neither functions.

**Puzzle sequence:**

- Inspect the lift route and identify the upper release.
- Restore the waterwheel.
- Ride the moving lift.
- Reach the upper walkway.
- Release a physical ladder into the present-day route.

When restoration fades, the lift stops. The released ladder remains.

The player uses an established ability in a different way: moving machinery rather than a bridge or swing.

There is no new monster here. This provides breathing space before finding Eli.

## Scene 10 — Three knocks

**Target:** Minutes 18–20.

**Objective:** Find Eli inside the tower.

An exterior service gallery reaches an iron-latticed inspection window overlooking the upper landing. The window allows sight and sound, but prevents passage or passing the ember carrier through.

The proper entrance is reached through the tower’s interior mechanisms.

Eli stands beyond the window.

Two knocks.

Rowan has followed recordings throughout the game. He tests this one.

> **Rowan:** “Copy me.”

He taps three times.

Eli answers three.

> **Eli:** “That’s too many.”

**The major recognition scene:** Eli is responding to something that never happened in the recorded past.

The player restores the nearby fire. Rowan tries to guide Eli toward the chamber’s inner doorway.

Eli takes a step. As restoration fades, he is pulled back into the interrupted position.

He remains aware. He remembers speaking to Rowan.

> **Eli:** “I thought I could get down after they left.”

This confirms what Nia heard. He rang the bell to draw danger away from their escape.

Rowan now connects Eli’s condition to Tomas’s experience.

> **Rowan:** “Mara brought Tomas back. I know what to bring.”

Before leaving, the player reaches the Old Road release in the courtyard and secures it permanently.

**New objective:** Take the last memory ember from home.

## Scene 11 — The last afternoon

**Target:** Minutes 20–22.

The Old Road returns Rowan to the refuge. From there, the cleared market leads home.

This journey contains no respawned enemies. It lets the player recognise their progress.

Nia’s optional bird can be returned here.

At home, Rowan restores the hearth.

The departure repeats exactly as it did at the beginning.

> **Eli’s recording:** “The tower today?”  
> **Remembered Rowan:** “After work. I promise.”

The recording does not turn toward Rowan or acquire new dialogue.

The interaction explains its consequence clearly:

> **Take the last memory ember.**  
> **This home’s remembered afternoon will no longer return.**

The player deliberately holds the interaction.

On completion:

- The unique ember enters its protected inventory slot.
- The home memory fades.
- The hearth becomes permanently spent.
- Ordinary illumination and resting remain available.
- The game saves the committed outcome.

> **Rowan:** “I’m coming now.”

**This is the emotional climax.** The player sacrifices access to a comforting reconstruction to help the real person waiting elsewhere.

There is no choice that secretly produces a better ending. The deliberate interaction gives the decision weight without inventing a branching morality system.

## Scene 12 — Inside the clock tower

**Target:** Minutes 22–25.

**Objective:** Open the upper landing.

The player returns through the unlocked Old Road.

Three mechanisms form a continuous ascent:

### The lift bell

Restore the lower machinery, ride the lift and ring the maintenance bell to secure its landing. A permanent ladder becomes available.

### The counterweight bell

Read the movement of a counterweight, cross onto its platform and reach the next mechanism. Securing it releases a permanent catwalk.

### The landing bell

Reach the upper controls and open the heavy entrance to Eli’s chamber.

The bells are attached to real mechanisms. Their purpose is visible. They are not three unexplained magical locks.

Each completed mechanism persists through retries.

The last opening exposes the approach occupied by the Bell Warden. A safe recovery point precedes the arena.

## Scene 13 — Final boss: the Bell Warden

**Target:** Minutes 25–28.

The Warden is a large corruption creature occupying the landing’s machinery. It blocks access to Eli; defeating it does not, by itself, anchor him.

The arena contains a restoration fire, a damaged counterweight and a clearly readable fighting space.

### Opening phase: break its protection

Frontal sweeps strike armour and give clear feedback.

The player must:

- Restore the counterweight mechanism.
- Position the Warden along its path.
- Bait its committed charge.
- Dodge so the charge collides with the mechanism.

The impact breaks its outer protection for the remainder of that attempt.

This combines the bridge elite’s charge lesson with the tower’s machinery.

### Exposed phase: earn attack windows

The Warden uses:

- A low shockwave that must be jumped.
- A forward lunge that must be dodged.
- A recovery period during which a lantern sweep can damage it.

Four successful sweeps defeat it. After two hits, its pattern adds a second, clearly signalled shockwave.

There are no random reinforcements or unannounced attacks from offscreen.

### Resource fairness

- The recovery point supplies the established capped resources.
- The oil vial provides room for a mistake.
- The player can retreat and reset the encounter.
- Death resets the boss attempt, but keeps secured tower mechanisms.
- No story dialogue repeats on every failure.

The Warden disperses. The route to Eli stays clear.

**This is the mechanical climax:** observation, restoration, positioning, dodging and attack work together.

## Scene 14 — What remains

**Target:** Minutes 28–30, including the ending.

The player enters Eli’s chamber.

The final interaction consumes the protected hearth ember. It does not require additional ordinary flame, so combat expenditure cannot block the ending.

Giving the ember briefly restores the chamber.

Daylight. Intact stone. Moving air.

Eli steps forward and takes Rowan’s hand.

Then the familiar fading effect begins.

The tower becomes ruined again.

**Eli stays.**

Give this visual proof a moment without dialogue.

The player then walks with him onto the viewing platform.

> **Eli:** “You can see our house.”  
> **Rowan:** “Yes.”

The village remains dark, with small surviving fires visible below.

The promised visit has happened under circumstances neither brother expected.

The Old Road provides a safe return. Eli has no escort health bar, and there is no final ambush.

At the refuge, Nia can briefly acknowledge him. At home, the brothers sit beside the ordinary hearth.

It cannot replay their afternoon.

Eli taps twice.

The player answers.

Credits.

## The consistent player mechanics

| System | Rule |
|---|---|
| Movement | Run, jump and climb; moving camera with enough look-ahead to read obstacles |
| Health | Three health segments; damage immunity prevents repeated contact damage |
| Ordinary illumination | Remains available at zero flame |
| Flame | Maximum 100; no passive drain while travelling |
| Restoration | Costs 25 on successful activation; active memories cannot be restarted or extended |
| Lantern sweep | Costs 20; directional range, anticipation and cooldown |
| Oil | One vial; restores 35 flame up to the cap |
| Rest | Deliberate two-second hold at a reachable fire; restores capped supplies and sets a safe checkpoint |
| Context interaction | Uses the action shown by the prompt; card selection and machinery interaction do not silently spend restoration fuel |
| Inventory | Pauses play; displays equipment, supplies and protected quest objects |
| Map and journal | Show known routes, current objective and essential evidence |
| Hearth ember | Unique, protected on death, separate from fuel and without a travel countdown |
| Progress | Opened routes, completed rescues, collected quest items and defeated bosses persist |
| Retries | Reset an unfinished encounter safely; do not replay long conversations or remove committed quest progress |

Resting repeatedly is permitted recovery. It cannot duplicate rewards, regenerate a spent hearth memory or create another quest ember.

Mandatory information appears on safe ground. No player should have to read a revelation while their crossing timer expires.

## Why every major task belongs in this story

- Freeing Mara opens the route and connects Rowan to the family Eli helped.
- Five-Hand turns an established village activity into a readable combat puzzle.
- Helping Tomas repairs the route and establishes how anchoring works.
- Speaking to Nia explains Eli’s destination and the evacuation.
- Searching the playground provides the carrier and service route.
- Finding Eli confirms that the rescue method is relevant.
- Returning home supplies the anchor at a demonstrated cost.
- Repairing the tower provides physical access.
- Defeating the Warden removes the final obstruction.
- Giving the ember resolves Eli’s condition.
- Sharing the view and returning home resolve the relationship.

## The three audit passes

### Audit 1 — Timeline

Pre-impact recordings establish ordinary life and old routes. Living witnesses and present evidence explain the evacuation. Eli’s responsive behaviour distinguishes him from a recording.

### Audit 2 — Motivation

Every required detour either opens the route, provides rescue knowledge or supplies something necessary. The family’s earlier rescue establishes the hearth cost before Rowan faces it.

### Audit 3 — Playability

There are two distinct bosses, one elite, two common enemy behaviours and several different traversal puzzles. Shortcuts limit repetition; persistent progress limits frustration. Actual duration, shuffle readability and boss difficulty remain things to establish through playtesting.

## Campaign acceptance principle

A connected rescue adventure where the player’s actions earn both the route forward and the understanding needed to finish it.

A fresh player should be able to explain why Eli went to the tower, why Rowan returned home, what the ember cost, and why Eli staying after daylight fades means the rescue succeeded.

## Route order changes

**Today:** playground cup → passage home → take ember → canal (needs ember) → tower bells → Warden → rescue.

**Campaign:** playground cup → canal → **tower courtyard: find Eli, three knocks, open the Old Road** → Old Road to the Refuge → market → **home: take the ember** → back through the Old Road → tower bells → Warden → rescue → viewing platform → Old Road home.

So the canal and the tower courtyard stop requiring the ember, the tower interior (bells) still requires it, and `eliFound` (not the cup) unlocks taking the ember at the hearth.
